Design Approach — Kiosk Check-In Flow

How should the visitor experience feel on a lobby tablet?

A

Full-Screen Wizard (Recommended)

Each step takes the entire screen. Huge touch targets, one action per screen. Progress bar at top. Back/Next navigation at bottom. Feels like a premium kiosk — think airport check-in or hotel lobby iPad.

Steps: Welcome → Name → Phone → Who to See → Reason → License Photo → Sign NDA → Confirmation

Pros

  • Dead simple for visitors
  • Perfect for tablets
  • Feels modern & premium
  • Easy to add/remove steps per client

Cons

  • More screens = more taps
  • More code to build
B

Card-Stack Wizard

Steps slide in as stacked cards from the bottom. Previous steps collapse behind. Visitor sees progress physically stacking up. Smooth animations between steps.

Flow: Same steps as A, but presented as layered cards with slide-up transitions

Pros

  • Visually engaging
  • Shows progress naturally
  • Unique/memorable

Cons

  • More complex animations
  • Can feel cluttered on small tablets
  • Harder to go back to earlier steps
C

Split View — Form + Live Log (Current, Refined)

Keep the current split layout but upgrade each side. Left: multi-step form (not wizard, but sectioned). Right: live visitor log. Both visible at once on landscape tablets.

Best for: Front desk staff who need to see the log while checking someone in

Pros

  • Dual-purpose screen
  • Less rework from current
  • Good for receptionist use

Cons

  • Cramped on portrait tablets
  • Not ideal for self-service
  • Doesn't feel like a premium kiosk

My Recommendation: A (Full-Screen Wizard)

Enterprise clients expect a polished, self-service experience. The full-screen wizard is the industry standard for visitor management SaaS (Envoy, SwipedOn, Proxyclick all use this pattern). It works for both self-service visitors AND receptionists, and the step-per-screen approach makes it trivial to add/remove steps per company later.
